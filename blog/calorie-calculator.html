    The short version

    Your daily calories are your resting energy times how active your day is, adjusted for your goal. Protein is set from your weight, fat is a fixed share of calories, carbohydrate is whatever is left, and fibre is a fixed amount. Each of those steps comes from a published guideline:

    The rest of this page goes through each step, with the equations and the reasoning, so you can check any number Bud gives you.

    Step 1How much energy you use at rest

    Your basal metabolic rate (BMR) is the energy your body uses just to keep running - breathing, circulation, keeping warm. It is most of what you burn in a day. The international standard for predicting it is the set of equations in the FAO/WHO/UNU expert report on human energy requirements3, which work from your weight, age and sex:

    AgeMen (kcal/day)Women (kcal/day)
    18–3015.057 × weight + 692.214.818 × weight + 486.6
    30–6011.472 × weight + 873.18.126 × weight + 845.6
    60 and over11.711 × weight + 587.79.082 × weight + 658.5

    Weight is in kilograms. An age of exactly 30 or 60 uses the older band.

    Then comes the Indian adjustment. When ICMR's National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR-NIN) reviewed its Nutrient Requirements for Indians in 2020, it found that these international equations overestimate the BMR of Indian adults by 10–12%. The data behind them came largely from young, muscular subjects, and Indians carry relatively more body fat at the same weight1. ICMR's method lowers the result by about 10%. Applied as 10% for men and 9% for women, it reproduces ICMR's own published requirements to the calorie, so that is exactly what Bud does:

    Resting energy = FAO/WHO/UNU equation × 0.90 (men) or × 0.91 (women) Under 18 there is no cut: ICMR found no evidence that Indian children burn less at rest, and kept the international figure.

    Step 2How active you are

    Your day's total is your resting energy multiplied by a physical activity level (PAL). ICMR-NIN recalculated these for India using the measured energy cost of activities in Indian studies, which came out lower than the Western figures: a sedentary day is 1.4, where it was 1.53 in ICMR's 2010 guidance1.

    Two of the four levels are ICMR's own figures and two are worked out between them. Most people who use Bud sit at a desk, and how often they work out is what separates them. The FAO/WHO/UNU report puts a sitting day with a little activity anywhere from 1.40 to 1.69, and notes that an hour of moderate to vigorous exercise a day raises a person's PAL by about 0.2, "from 1.55 to 1.75"3. So "lightly active" is 1.5 and "moderately active" is ICMR's 1.4 plus that 0.2, both inside that band; and "very active" - working out regularly - is 1.8, ICMR's moderate work and inside FAO/WHO/UNU's active band of 1.70–1.99. ICMR's heavy-work figure, 2.3, is a labourer's day, and Bud doesn't use it: on a desk job with regular workouts it would put your calories about 30% too high.

    A check against ICMR's own table. For a 65 kg man this method gives 2,110 kcal a day sedentary and 2,710 for moderate work ("very active" here), and for a 55 kg woman 1,660 and 2,130 - ICMR-NIN's published reference requirements exactly1. (At ICMR's heavy-work factor it gives 3,460 against ICMR's 3,470 for men, and 2,720 exactly for women.)

    Step 3Your goal

    The figure from step 2 is what keeps your weight where it is. Your goal then moves it:

    GoalCaloriesSource

    Weight loss500 kcal a day below maintenance, about half a kilo a weekAHA/ACC/TOS6
    Diabetes careThe weight-loss target if your BMI is 25 or more; maintenance if it's lowerADA Standards of Care11
    Muscle building10% above maintenanceIraki et al.10
    GLP-1 medication
    (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro…)    		500 kcal below maintenance, never under 1,200 (women) or 1,800 (men)Joint GLP-1 advisory7

    Weight loss. The 2013 AHA/ACC/TOS obesity guideline - one of its strongest recommendations - prescribes a deficit of 500 or 750 kcal a day, or a fixed intake of 1,200–1,500 kcal for women and 1,500–1,800 for men6. Bud uses the smaller deficit, because a pace you can keep for months beats a fast first fortnight, and never sets a target below the bottom of those ranges: 1,200 kcal for women and 1,500 for men.

    Diabetes care. For somebody with type 2 diabetes who is overweight, losing 5% or more of their weight is a first-line recommendation of the American Diabetes Association's Standards of Care11. At a healthy weight there is nothing to lose, so the target is maintenance.

    GLP-1 medication. These drugs suppress appetite so effectively that the usual problem is eating too little and losing muscle along with fat. The 2025 joint advisory from four obesity and nutrition societies warns that intakes below 1,200 kcal a day for women or 1,800 for men risk falling short on vitamins and minerals7, so those are the floors.

    Muscle building. A review of nutrition for natural lifters recommends a surplus of about 10–20% over maintenance10. Bud uses the lower end, because a bigger surplus adds fat faster rather than muscle.

    Nobody with a BMI under 18.5, and nobody under 18, is given a deficit, whatever goal they pick. If you pick several goals, your calories follow one of them - GLP-1 first, then weight loss, diabetes care and muscle building - and protein takes the highest figure among the goals you picked.

    Step 4Protein

    Goalg per kgSource
    General nutrition, and diabetes care at a healthy weight1.0ICMR-NIN 20201
    Weight loss, and diabetes care with a BMI of 25 or more1.21.2–1.6 during weight loss7
    GLP-1 medication1.4Joint GLP-1 advisory7
    Muscle building1.6ISSN8, Morton et al.9

    ICMR-NIN's recommended allowance for Indian adults is 0.83 g of protein per kg of body weight. For people whose diet is mostly cereal, with lower-quality protein, it is 1 g per kg1 - which describes most Indian plates, so that is Bud's figure for general nutrition. While you're losing weight, more protein helps you lose fat rather than muscle, and 1.2–1.6 g per kg is the range the 2025 GLP-1 advisory cites for that7. For building muscle, the International Society of Sports Nutrition recommends 1.4–2.0 g per kg8, and a meta-analysis of 49 trials found no extra gain beyond about 1.69.

    Protein is worked out on a healthy weight. Muscle, not body fat, is what sets how much protein you need, so working it out on your total weight overstates it for anyone carrying extra fat - a point the GLP-1 advisory makes directly7. Bud uses your weight or the top of the healthy range for your height (a BMI of 24.9), whichever is lower.

    Protein (g) = g per kg × the lower of: your weight, or 24.9 × height(m)²

    Step 5Fat, carbohydrate and fibre

    Fat is 25% of your calories. WHO's 2023 guideline recommends that adults get 30% of their energy from fat or less, mostly from unsaturated fats, with saturated fat under 10%5. ICMR-NIN's 2024 Dietary Guidelines for Indians set the same 30% ceiling2. A quarter sits comfortably inside it.

    Carbohydrate is what is left once protein (4 kcal a gram) and fat (9 kcal a gram) are taken out of your calories. Protein and fat are set first because a deficit should never squeeze out protein. WHO's 2023 carbohydrate guideline puts the emphasis on where carbohydrate comes from: whole grains, vegetables, fruit and pulses4. ICMR-NIN's guidelines add that cereals should provide no more than 45% of your energy, with half of them whole grains, and that added sugar should stay under 5%2.

    Fibre is 25 g a day, WHO's recommended minimum of naturally occurring fibre for adults4. It is part of your carbohydrate rather than counted on top of it.

    Fat (g) = calories × 0.25 ÷ 9 Carbs (g) = (calories − protein × 4 − fat × 9) ÷ 4 Fibre = 25 g

    A worked example

    A 32-year-old woman, 160 cm and 70 kg, who sits at a desk most of the day and wants to lose weight:

    1. Resting energy: 8.126 × 70 + 845.6 = 1,414 kcal, less 9% = 1,287 kcal.
    2. Mostly sitting: 1,287 × 1.4 = 1,802 kcal to stay at 70 kg.
    3. Weight loss: 1,802 − 500 = 1,302, rounded to 1,300 kcal.
    4. Protein: the top of her healthy range is 24.9 × 1.6² = 63.7 kg, below her weight, so 1.2 × 63.7 = 76 g.
    5. Fat: 1,300 × 0.25 ÷ 9 = 36 g. Carbs: (1,300 − 304 − 324) ÷ 4 = 168 g. Fibre: 25 g.

    Her BMI is 27.3, which WHO classes as overweight; the healthy range for her height is 47–64 kg. Put the same details in the calculator above and you'll get the same five numbers.

    What these numbers can't do

    • They're a starting point. Any equation predicts the average for people like you, and an individual can be 10% either side. If your weight hasn't moved the way you expected after three or four weeks of honest logging, the target needs adjusting - tell Bud, or your dietitian.
    • They're for healthy adults. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, kidney disease, a history of disordered eating and many medications change what you need. Follow your doctor's or dietitian's advice over any calculator.
    • A dietitian's numbers come first. If you work with a dietitian through Bud and they set your targets, Bud uses theirs, not these.

    Common questions

    How many calories does an Indian adult need a day?

    ICMR-NIN's 2020 reference figures are 2,110 kcal a day for a sedentary 65 kg man and 1,660 kcal for a sedentary 55 kg woman, rising to 2,710 and 2,130 for moderate work1. Your own figure depends on your age, sex, weight and how active you are - the calculator at the top of this page works it out with the same method.

    Why are these numbers lower than other calorie calculators?

    Most online calculators use equations built on Western data. ICMR-NIN found that the international equations overestimate the resting energy use of Indian adults by 10–12%, and that Indians use less energy for the same activities. So its method cuts resting energy by about 10% and uses an activity factor of 1.4, not 1.53, for a sedentary day1.

    How much protein do I need a day?

    ICMR-NIN's recommended allowance is 0.83 g per kg of body weight, and 1 g per kg on a cereal-based diet1. During weight loss 1.2–1.6 g per kg helps keep muscle7, and for building muscle the range is 1.4–2.08. Bud works it out on your weight, or on the top of the healthy range for your height if you're above it.

    How big a calorie deficit should I use to lose weight?

    About 500 kcal a day below what you burn, which comes to roughly half a kilo a week. That's the smaller of the two deficits the AHA/ACC/TOS obesity guideline recommends6, and Bud never sets a target below 1,200 kcal for women or 1,500 for men.

    How much fat and fibre should I eat?

    WHO recommends fat at 30% of your daily energy or less, mostly unsaturated5, and at least 25 g of naturally occurring fibre a day for adults4. Bud sets fat at 25% of calories and fibre at 25 g.

    Is this medical advice?

    No. These are general targets for healthy adults, worked out from published guidelines. If you're pregnant or breastfeeding, under 18, managing a medical condition or taking medication, follow your doctor's or dietitian's advice.
