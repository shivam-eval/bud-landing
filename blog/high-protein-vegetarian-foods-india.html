How to get more protein on an Indian vegetarian diet

Getting enough protein as a vegetarian doesn't have to mean drinking protein shakes or eating paneer at every meal.

By the Bud team · Published 9 November 2026

If you eat regular Indian food, you probably already have some good protein sources in your kitchen. The trick is knowing which foods to add and how much you need.

Step 1Start with these everyday protein sources

Here are some easy options to work into your meals:

FoodApprox. protein
Soya chunks, 50 g dry25 g
Paneer, 100 g18 g
Tofu, 150 g12–18 g
Besan, 60 g12 g
Cooked dal, 1 cup7–10 g
Curd, 200 g6–8 g
Milk, 250 ml8 g
Roasted chana, 30 g6 g

These are estimates, not exact numbers2. The type of food, brand, recipe and portion size can change the protein content.

Step 2Upgrade the meals you already eat

You don't need an entirely new diet. Start with small swaps.

  • Breakfast: Make besan or moong dal chillas and add some curd.
  • Lunch: Keep your roti or rice, sabzi and dal. Just make sure your dal portion is substantial.
  • Snack: Keep roasted chana, milk or curd handy instead of relying on biscuits or namkeen every day.
  • Dinner: Try paneer bhurji, tofu stir-fry or soya keema with your usual roti and vegetables.

Notice the pattern? You're adding protein to familiar meals, not replacing everything you enjoy.

Step 3Do you need protein powder?

Not necessarily. A varied vegetarian diet that includes pulses, beans, soy, dairy, grains, nuts and seeds can provide protein without supplements. The ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians1 discuss the importance of dietary variety and adequate pulses in vegetarian diets.

Protein powder can be convenient, especially when you're busy, but it isn't a requirement for everyone.

Step 4How much protein do you actually need?

There isn't one target that works for everyone. Your needs depend on factors such as your body size, activity level and goals.

Rather than copying a number from social media, start with a personal estimate using Bud's calorie and macro calculator. If you have a medical condition that affects your diet, check your target with a qualified dietitian or doctor.

The takeaway: You don't need a perfect diet. Start by adding a reliable protein source to your regular meals, make the habit easy to repeat, and build from there.
